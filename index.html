About the Project

Bringing Literature,
Technology and Livelihood!

This ICSSR funded study investigates the potential of affordable IoT based monitoring system to enable sustainable mushroom cultivation as a livelihood option for rural women who currently have no awareness or access of it.

The Problem

Mushroom cultivation offers significant business potential, yet the majority of rural women in our study area had never tried it. Commercial IoT monitoring tools that improve yield reliability cost ₹5,000–20,000, which can be inaccessible to women farmers.

Our Approach

We first went to the field to understand the reality faced by women farmers before designing any technology. This approach ensures our solution addresses real barriers rather than assumed ones.

The Solution

A plug-and-play IoT monitoring kit that requires no technical knowledge to set up. Any woman with a basic smartphone can connect it via a simple captive portal with no IT support and technical training needed.

Project Objectives

  1. Gathering baseline awareness and feasibility of mushroom cultivation among rural women in Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan
  2. Design and validate an affordable IoT monitoring kit appropriate for non-technical rural users
  3. Identify socioeconomic barriers to technology adoption and propose appropriate solutions
  4. Establish a scalable deployment model suitable for Self Help Groups and cooperative settings
  5. Contribute to the evidence base for livelihood interventions targeting rural women in India
Why This Matters?

The Challenges, We Set Out to Solve:

Our field interactions across four states revealed a consistent fact that women eager for income opportunities are facing socio-economic barriers.

Zero Awareness

The majority of women we met had either little or no knowledge that mushrooms could be cultivated commercially at home. The awareness gap is more difficult to overcome than technology barrier.

Cost Factor

Commercial mushroom monitoring systems cost approximately ₹5,000–20,000. For a household earning ₹8,000–12,000 per month, this is completely inaccessible without subsidy.

Digital Literacy Gap

Most existing IoT agricultural tools require significant technical setup. Women with basic smartphone access cannot deploy them without external technical support.

Connectivity Limits

Rural areas across our study states have variable internet access. A solution that fails when the WiFi is unstable is not a reliable livelihood tool.

Research Gap

A systematic review of literature found no published study addressing IoT mushroom cultivation in the context of women's empowerment or rural Indian deployment.

Policy Disconnect

NABARD and state government schemes promote mushroom cultivation for SHGs but lack low-cost monitoring tools to support consistent and reliable yields at scale.

The Technology

A Kit Built Around Real Constraints:

Every design decision was made in direct response to what we heard in the field. The result is a monitoring kit that works for the people who are in need of it.

IoT Monitoring Kit

Temperature · Humidity · CO₂

  • Plug and play WiFi setup No technical knowledge needed. Phone connects to kit hotspot, user picks their WiFi network. Done.
  • Real-time sensor monitoring Temperature, humidity and CO₂ monitored continuously. Alerts sent to Android app when conditions go outside safe range.
  • Automated relay control Humidifier activates automatically when humidity drops. No manual intervention required during grow cycles.
  • Data recording and export All sensor readings stored and exportable as CSV for farmer records, loan applications or cooperative reporting.

Kit cost per unit

~₹1,500

Commercial equivalent: ₹15,000–50,000

Up to 97% more affordable
